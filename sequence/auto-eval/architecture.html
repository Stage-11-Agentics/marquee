Autonomous Eval Execution

How Marquee scores itself against sbek, triages what the harness finds, fixes it, and fires the next round — with no operator in the seat. Runs in the c11 workspace M: Auto Eval.

The finding it is built around

The distance from the current score to 100% is not a discovery problem. Every non-pass rubric item is already on disk, carrying the judge's own reasoning for why it fell short, and the spec carries that item's pass_criteria. Together those are a finished ticket, written by the thing that grades us.

So rounds are not the engine. The queue is the engine; rounds verify that conversions landed and catch what moved backward.

The coverage trap

cannot_judge sits outside the denominator; not_found sits inside it at zero. An unreached item therefore costs nothing today and costs real points the moment a round reaches it and finds nothing there. Capabilities get built before a round can reach them — otherwise coverage repair shows up as a regression the loop caused by improving itself.

Topology — who talks to whom

Two machines, and the split is deliberate. The eval browses on Atlas; the fleet builds on Hyperion. Implementers and gate runs therefore never perturb the eval's browser timing — and since speed is a graded rubric item, a laggy page under fleet load would score as a product defect.

Once fired, the run is unreachable

The eval agent on Atlas is a separate headless session on its own credential — not a subagent of anything here. The coordinator can observe it, because the mission requires a PROGRESS.log line after every scenario, but it cannot steer it. No mid-flight correction, no "hold on, I'm deploying." That is why the mission file and submissionNotes carry so much weight: they are the only instructions that run will ever receive.

The cycle

Why the barrier exists

A round measured against three different builds cannot be diffed against the next one, and attribution is the entire value the loop produces. Round 4 detected its own BUILD-DRIFT mid-flight and continued — correct behaviour, and exactly the thing the barrier removes the need for.

Triage — the claim that must not be wrong

Every backward move is exactly one of three things, and only the first is an emergency. Item-level always: aggregate counts hide compensating moves in both directions, and "the area regressed" is the claim most likely to send the fleet chasing nothing.

ClassWhat happenedResponse
(a) code regression A change that landed this round broke it. Ticket now, top of queue, name the PR.
(b) state-dependent surface The control wasn't on the page because earlier scenarios changed the state that renders it. The live case: a Remind button rendered only when outstanding_count > 0, after the round drove reviewers to completion. Ticket at normal priority. A capability that vanishes exactly when it isn't applicable is undiscoverable — render it disabled, with the count.
(c) judge variance Unchanged behaviour, read more harshly, or a control never exercised at all. Do not ticket. Watch item; becomes a ticket only if the next round repeats it.

The guards

These are what make it safe to leave running.

GuardWhy
Score floor + rollback anchor A round may lose 2 points before the loop halts, stops deploying, and flags. The best-scoring sha is kept so the answer to a bad round is always "go back."
Deploy only at the barrier Attribution. Merging stays continuous.
One gate at a time Same branch, one night: load 14 → 78s, load 48 → 135s, load 164 → 276s against a 120s budget — and all three passed. Serializing keeps the warns honest; it was never protection against false reds, because slowness cannot red the gate. A red is load-invariant.
Deadline barrier, not completion barrier Never wait on "all tickets done" — one stuck implementer would end the run. Unfinished tickets roll forward.
Migrations stay gated A bad merge costs a git revert. A migration applied to the live D1 does not come back that way.
Reset proven, not assumed The barrier refuses to fire unless the landing page reads "AI Engineer New York 2026" — a round against polluted state produces ghost tickets.

The second win condition

sbek scores 98 rubric items and has no opinion about whether Marquee is a pleasure to use. Clear every lane above and you have a product tuned to a rubric. The craft critic runs in parallel against PHILOSOPHY.md and DESIGN.md, walking whole organizer tasks rather than screens and judging the seams between steps — where a rubric never looks and a human always feels it.

Where the two axes converge

Discoverability. A control that is present but hard to find is simultaneously a craft defect and a scoring defect, because a judge cannot score a button it cannot see. That overlap is the richest vein in the queue, and the critic is told to spend its time there first.

Escalation

The coordinator wakes the operator for exactly two things: the score floor tripping (it has already rolled back to the anchor by then) and a migration being required. Everything else — a failed gate, a stuck implementer, a judge that disagrees with an analyst — it handles, and reports in its c11 description so the state of the run is readable off the sidebar without typing anything.
