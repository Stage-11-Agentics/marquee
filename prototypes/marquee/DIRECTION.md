# Direction: The Marquee

**Read `prototypes/PROTOTYPE-CONTRACT.md` first — it is binding. This file is what makes your take different.**

## Core assumption

**The agenda is the product.** A conference *is* its program board — the thing on the marquee. Everything else (CFP, review, speakers) is a feeder to the board, and the board is where quality becomes visible: balance across tracks, no conflicts, a program you can feel proud of at a glance.

## Home surface

The **schedule canvas**, full-bleed: the **track swimlane view** front and center (the view no competitor ships — tracks as lanes, days as bands), toggling to day/room/week/list. An **unscheduled pool** docked left (accepted sessions ready to place, filterable). Feeder panels — submissions, review status, speakers — dock as collapsible side panes around the canvas; the canvas never leaves the screen.

## Signature moves (must actually work)

1. **Drag-and-drop scheduling**: drag from the pool onto a lane/slot; duration defaults from format; drag between slots; conflicts (room overlap, double-booked person) flag live on the tiles and in a conflicts drawer.
2. **The five-view toggle** with fixed-width segmented control (no layout shift), filters and scroll position preserved across views.
3. **Program balance meter**: live counts by track/format/day beside the canvas; a lopsided program is visible while it's still fixable.

## Aesthetic & voice

**Bold editorial — theatrical, worthy of the name.** A marquee-inspired identity: strong display typography for the brand and event name, high-contrast dark canvas where the schedule tiles carry the color (track colors as the palette), generous tile design that makes the program feel like a poster being composed. Motion only in direct manipulation (drag feels physical). Voice: curatorial — "Tuesday's Agents track runs heavy. Two lightning slots open Wednesday." The tool treats program-building as a craft.
